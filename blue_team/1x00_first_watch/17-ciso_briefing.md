# MedDefense Health Systems — CISO Board Briefing

## Opening Statement

MedDefense is operating with a high-risk, fragmented security posture: important controls exist, but critical systems and patient data remain exposed when one control fails. The concern is not one isolated weakness; it is how several weaknesses can combine into a major clinical incident.

## Critical Finding

The most dangerous gap is the lack of effective separation between user devices, servers and medical equipment. If one system is compromised, an attacker could move toward the EHR, Active Directory, imaging systems or connected medical devices, turning a single foothold into a wider outage, privacy breach or patient-safety event.

## Three Priority Actions

**1. Close known entry points now — $48,000, within 1 month.** Fix and verify patient-portal access controls, replace Westside’s consumer router, isolate the legacy MRI, secure the network closet and restrict server-room access.

**2. Contain compromise — $43,000, staged over more than 1 month.** Separate user, server and medical-device networks and allow only required communications between them.

**3. Detect and recover faster — remaining $29,000, begin within 1 month.** Start centralized security monitoring and strengthen isolated backups and incident-response readiness.

## Business Case

The $120,000 budget is risk reduction, not overhead. MedDefense has already suffered a four-day billing outage from ransomware; a comparable healthcare breach in our review caused an eleven-day EHR outage.

## Closing

Without action, MedDefense remains one successful intrusion away from preventable disruption to patient care, sensitive data and core hospital operations.
