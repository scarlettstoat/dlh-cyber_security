# MedDefense Health Systems — Asset Criticality Assessment

The following assessment groups the assets identified in the Task 7 Asset Registry into ten logical categories and evaluates each category against the Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability (CIA) triad.

The rating scale is:

- **Critical** — compromise directly threatens patient safety, causes regulatory violation or halts clinical operations.
- **High** — compromise causes significant operational disruption, financial loss or data exposure.
- **Medium** — compromise causes moderate disruption that can be recovered through standard procedures.
- **Low** — compromise has minimal operational or security impact.

The **Overall Criticality** rating is the highest rating assigned across the three CIA pillars.

# Top 5 Most Critical Assets

## 1. EHR System — `ehr-srv-01`, `ehr-db-01` and EHR Application

The EHR system ranks first because it is the central clinical information source used by physicians and other care teams. MedDefense has already demonstrated its dependence on this service: a planned migration caused a **nine-hour outage**, forcing physicians to use paper records. A confidentiality breach would expose sensitive patient records, unauthorized modification could cause clinicians to rely on inaccurate information, and an outage removes immediate electronic access to information required during treatment. The combination of direct patient-safety impact, privacy exposure and organization-wide clinical dependency makes the EHR MedDefense's most critical asset.

## 2. Pharmacy Management System — `A-038`

The pharmacy management system ranks second because its integrity directly affects medication safety across **all three MedDefense sites**. During the March incident, a faulty database update caused incorrect dosages to be displayed for approximately **six hours**, and the problem was discovered only because a pharmacist compared the values with a printed reference. This is a demonstrated—not hypothetical—example of how incorrect system data could reach clinical staff and potentially affect patient treatment. Integrity and availability of this system therefore have immediate patient-safety consequences.

## 3. BD Alaris Infusion-Pump Estate — `A-032`

MedDefense operates approximately **120 network-connected BD Alaris infusion pumps** used for medication delivery and dosage updates. These devices rank third because manipulation or loss of availability can affect treatment being delivered directly to patients. The asset registry also records known vulnerabilities and the absence of recommended network isolation, while the devices remain reachable within Central's unsegmented internal environment. Their scale, direct connection to medication delivery and current exposure make the infusion-pump estate one of MedDefense's most safety-sensitive assets.

## 4. Network Core and Connectivity — FortiGate and Central Core Switching

The FortiGate and Central network core rank fourth because multiple critical services depend on them simultaneously. EHR systems, medical devices, workstations, imaging systems and authentication services all use the Central network, while Westside and Corporate HQ also depend on VPN connectivity back to Central. The scan confirmed that the apparent workstation, server and medical-device subnets are not enforced security segments, meaning a network-level compromise can affect several otherwise separate asset classes. A core failure or malicious configuration change could therefore create a multi-system clinical outage rather than an isolated technical problem.

## 5. Active Directory Service — `ad-dc-01` and `ad-dc-02`

Active Directory ranks fifth because it is a shared trust and authentication dependency across the MedDefense environment. Compromise of AD could allow unauthorized privilege changes and provide access paths into clinical and administrative systems, while loss of the service could prevent staff from authenticating to resources required during normal operations. The presence of two domain controllers provides some availability resilience, but it does not reduce the impact of a directory-wide compromise. Its role as the organization's centralized identity layer gives it a broader potential blast radius than most individual servers.
