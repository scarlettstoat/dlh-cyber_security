# MedDefense Health Systems — Attack Surface Map

## Scope

An **attack surface** is every point where an attacker can interact with MedDefense systems, data or people. It is broader than a vulnerability: an exposed service, login page or employee is part of the attack surface even when no confirmed weakness has yet been identified.

This map uses the Project 1x00 Network Scan findings, Asset Registry, Complete Control Matrix and Gap Analysis. Where the supplied evidence does not establish an exact port, management address or Internet exposure, that uncertainty is recorded rather than inferred.

### External-Surface Evidence Boundary

The supplied Asset Registry and consolidated scan evidence do **not** confirm additional internal hosts as directly Internet-reachable. `billing-srv-01` has an Apache/web-layer compromise history, but its exact original entry path and Internet exposure are not proven, so it is **not** classified here as a confirmed external service. The two unidentified Linux systems (`UNKNOWN-01` and the Westside unknown host) were discovered on internal site networks and are treated in the Internal Surface below.

# Section 2 — Internal Surface

## Central Finding: The Flat Network

The most important internal-surface finding is **GAP-001 — no effective internal segmentation between servers, workstations and medical devices**. The scan uses `10.10.1.0/24`, `10.10.2.0/24` and `10.10.3.0/24` addressing ranges, but these are addressing conventions rather than enforced security zones. An attacker who gains one internal foothold can therefore interact with a much larger set of systems than the addressing scheme suggests.

|---|---|---|---|

### Internal-Surface Pattern

The danger is not any single port in isolation. PostgreSQL, MySQL, SMB, DICOM, SSH, NAS management and medical-device interfaces become much more significant because **one compromised endpoint can attempt to reach many of them without passing through an enforced internal security boundary**. GAP-001 therefore multiplies the impact of weaknesses elsewhere.

# Section 3 — Human Surface

The human surface consists of people whose legitimate access, authority, workflow or physical position makes them useful targets for social engineering.

| Human Target | What They Can Access / Influence | Why They Are Targetable | Existing Control / Gap Increasing Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clinical staff — physicians, nurses, Radiology, Pharmacy** | EHR and patient records; PACS/imaging; pharmacy workflows; medical devices; clinical workstations | They need rapid access to patient data and are trained to respond quickly to urgent clinical requests. Busy shifts, urgency and helpfulness make phishing, vishing and credential requests more believable. Shared Radiology credentials also normalise poor identity practice. | **C-023** annual security-awareness training exists, but no ongoing testing/effectiveness metrics are documented; this task also identifies low training completion as a concern. **GAP-007** weak identity/MFA coverage and **GAP-011** weak continuous monitoring increase the consequence of credential theft or misuse. |
| **Reception / registration / front-desk staff** | Patient registration information and EHR access appropriate to their role; visitor-facing physical access point and frequent contact with patients, vendors and callers | They are the first point of contact for unfamiliar people and handle urgent requests continuously. Attackers can exploit helpfulness, impersonation and authority to request information, password resets or physical entry. | **C-019** visitor registration/badge verification and **C-023** awareness training provide partial protection. Physical weaknesses identified in **GAP-004/GAP-005** show that trusted-looking people can still gain opportunities near Critical infrastructure if challenge procedures fail. **GAP-011** also limits rapid detection of inappropriate EHR use. |
| **IT staff / help desk / administrators** | Active Directory, servers, network devices, VPNs, endpoints, backups and privileged credentials | They have the most technically valuable access. A relatively small IT team faces ticket pressure and fatigue, making urgent vendor requests, fake support calls and credential-reset pretexts especially effective. | Strong controls exist on selected systems, including **C-004 to C-008** on `ehr-srv-01`, but **GAP-007** shows MFA/PAM are not broadly implemented and **GAP-011** shows privileged activity is not continuously correlated. |
| **Executives — CEO, CFO and senior leadership** | Strategic information, financial approvals, sensitive email and authority over high-value transactions | Attackers exploit authority and confidentiality. Executives are high-value BEC targets because a believable message from the CEO or CFO can cause staff to bypass normal questioning or payment workflows. | **C-023** provides general awareness, but no 1x00 control specifically addresses BEC, executive impersonation or dual approval for unusual payments. **GAP-007** remains relevant where cloud/identity MFA is not broadly documented. |
| **External contractors and vendor personnel** | Access varies by role: MedTech has EHR maintenance access; Siemens services the MRI environment; other contractors may receive network, application or physical access | Their accounts, laptops and support processes sit partly outside MedDefense's direct control. Attackers can compromise a trusted third party and inherit the trust relationship. | **General administrative staff at HQ** | O365 email/files, HR/Finance/Legal/Marketing information and access to Central resources over the HQ site VPN | Routine business communication creates constant exposure to phishing, fake invoices, malicious attachments and brand impersonation. # Surface Assessment Summary

**The Internal Surface represents MedDefense's greatest risk today because it acts as the multiplier for failures on the other two surfaces.** External services and employees provide plausible initial footholds, but Project 1x00 showed that once an attacker is inside Central, the supposed workstation, server and medical-device subnets are not actually enforced security zones. The attacker can encounter network-wide PostgreSQL and MySQL exposure, reachable NAS management, Active Directory services, legacy Windows systems, medical-IoT interfaces and unmanaged Shadow IT. **GAP-001** therefore turns a single phished user, vulnerable web service or compromised vendor account into a potential route toward several Critical clinical assets. The risk is compounded by **GAP-011**, because fragmented/manual monitoring reduces the chance that this internal movement is detected quickly. The highest-value reduction in attack surface is therefore to enforce internal segmentation and restrict management/data services to only the systems and users that genuinely require them, while external hardening and human-focused controls reduce the number of footholds that reach that internal surface in the first place.
