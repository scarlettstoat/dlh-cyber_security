## The Defense Blueprint - Underpinned by Gold

Now that we know what MedHealth Systems has (Project 0x00), who threatens it (Project 0x01) and where the cracks are (Project 0x02). The question they have been waiting to ask is the one that matters most:

What do we do about it, and how do we know it is worth the money ?


This is where most security programs fail. Not because the analysis was wrong, but because the strategy was never built. The SANS 2024 Security Budget Survey found that 62% of organizations make security investment decisions based on "vendor recommendations" or "industry trends" rather than quantitative risk analysis tied to their own threat landscape. They buy tools because a sales engineer was persuasive, not because the math justified the spend. Then they are surprised when the next breach hits the exact gap they never funded.

This project marks a fundamental shift. You are no longer diagnosing. You are prescribing. Every recommendation you make must answer three questions that the CFO will ask: How much does it cost ? What happens if we do not do it ? And how do you know ?

You will learn the frameworks that structure defensive strategy (NIST CSF 2.0, CIS Controls, ISO 27001), not as academic knowledge but as tools you apply to MedDefense's specific situation. You will calculate risk quantitatively using real numbers (SLE, ARO, ALE), not colored squares on a matrix. You will build a formal Risk Register that a CISO can present to the Board. You will select and justify controls using cost-benefit analysis, draft an actual security policy, and design a 6-month roadmap with dependencies and milestones.

Why It Matters
Security professionals who can only find problems are analysts. Security professionals who can find problems AND design cost-justified solutions are architects. Architects get promoted.

The Security Strategy Document you produce here is the type of deliverable that launches security programs. When a CISO walks into a Board meeting and says, "For $120,000, I can reduce our ransomware exposure by 80% and our regulatory risk from non-compliant to audit-ready," that statement is backed by exactly the kind of work you are about to do.

## Context
Week four at MedDefense Health Systems.

Three reports now sit in a binder on James Chen's desk: the Security Posture Assessment, the Threat Landscape Report and the Vulnerability Assessment Summary. The Board has read all three. Last Friday, Dr. Morales (CEO) sent a one-line email to James:

"We understand the problem. Now show us the plan."

James gathers you and Sarah Park (IT Director) in the conference room. On the whiteboard, he has written three questions:

What do we fix first and why ?

How much will it cost vs. how much do we lose if we do not ?

How do we know this is the right plan ?

He turns to you. "We have $120,000. Not a dollar more. The CEO wants a plan she can present at the next Board meeting: a prioritized list of actions, the cost of each one, the expected reduction in risk, and a timeline. She wants to know that the money is being spent on the right things, in the right order, for the right reasons."

He adds: "And Robert Kim, the CFO, will challenge every number. He does not care about 'best practices.' He cares about return on investment. If you cannot show him the math, he will not sign the check."

Sarah Park leans in: "We also need a framework. We cannot keep making security decisions on intuition. The auditors asked which framework we follow. I said 'none formally.' That was not a good answer."

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this project, you are expected to be able to explain to anyone, without the help of Google:

Security Governance

The purpose and structure of NIST CSF 2.0 (six functions, categories, profiles)

How CIS Controls v8 are organized (18 controls, 3 Implementation Groups) and how they complement NIST CSF

The role of ISO 27001 in security management systems and how it relates to NIST CSF and CIS Controls

What governance structures a security program requires: roles (data owner, controller, processor, custodian), RACI matrices, policy hierarchies

How to assess an organization's current maturity against a framework and define a target profile

Risk Management

How to calculate quantitative risk: Asset Value, Exposure Factor, SLE, ARO, ALE

How to use ALE to compare the cost of a control against the cost of the risk it addresses

The four risk treatment strategies (mitigate, transfer, accept, avoid) and when each is appropriate

How to build a formal Risk Register with ownership, treatment decisions and key risk indicators

How risk appetite and risk tolerance shape security investment decisions

Mitigation Techniques

How to select and justify specific security controls from CIS Controls for identified risks

How to perform cost-benefit analysis on proposed controls

How to design a security roadmap with dependencies, quick wins and long-term initiatives

How to draft an Acceptable Use Policy grounded in identified risks and controls

Professional Communication

How to defend technical security recommendations to a non-technical CFO using business language

How to produce a Security Strategy Document that synthesizes analysis into action

How to communicate trade-offs when budget forces prioritization
