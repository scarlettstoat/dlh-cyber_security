## 1. Purpose

This Incident Response Policy establishes how GlobalTech Manufacturing identifies, reports, assesses, contains, eradicates, recovers from, and learns from cybersecurity incidents.

The objective is to reduce the impact of incidents on business operations, manufacturing systems, IoT/OT environments, employees, customers, and company data while supporting compliance with ISO 27001, GDPR, and applicable industry-specific requirements.

GlobalTech follows the incident response lifecycle below:

> **Preparation → Detection & Analysis → Containment / Eradication / Recovery → Post-Incident**

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 Applicability

This policy applies to:

- [x] All employees
- [x] Contractors and consultants
- [x] Third-party vendors with authorised access
- [x] IT and security personnel
- [x] Manufacturing and OT personnel
- [x] Remote workers

### 2.2 Systems/Assets Covered

This policy applies to incidents affecting:

- Corporate networks and endpoints
- Servers and cloud services
- Email and collaboration systems
- Manufacturing systems
- Operational Technology (OT) and Industrial Control Systems (ICS)
- IoT devices and sensors
- Remote access and VPN services
- Identity and access systems
- Business applications and databases
- Personal data and confidential company information
- Third-party systems where GlobalTech data or operations may be affected

### 2.3 Exclusions

There are no general exclusions for cybersecurity incidents affecting GlobalTech systems, operations, or data.

Events that are confirmed not to create a security impact may be closed as non-incidents after assessment.

Any exception to this policy must follow the exception process in Section 10.

## 3. Incident Classification

Incidents must be classified according to business impact, operational impact, data sensitivity, legal or regulatory impact, number of affected users, and potential safety consequences.

### 3.1 Incident Classification Matrix

| Severity | Description | Initial Response Time | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | Incident causing or likely to cause severe business disruption, major safety risk, widespread compromise, major data breach, or significant regulatory impact. | **Within 15 minutes** | Ransomware affecting production; compromise of critical OT/ICS systems; confirmed large-scale personal data breach; destructive malware; active attacker with privileged access; cyber incident creating physical safety risk. |
| **High** | Serious incident with significant impact to important systems, sensitive data, or multiple business units, but not yet causing organisation-wide disruption. | **Within 1 hour** | Compromised administrator account; malware on several systems; confirmed unauthorised access to confidential data; major phishing campaign with successful compromise; significant production-system outage caused by cyber activity. |
| **Medium** | Incident with limited impact that can be contained using normal security and IT procedures. | **Within 4 hours** | Malware on one workstation; limited account compromise; unauthorised software with security impact; suspicious internal scanning; loss of a company device containing protected data. |
| **Low** | Minor event with little or no confirmed business impact and low risk of escalation. | **Within 1 business day** | Blocked phishing attempt; unsuccessful login attacks; policy violation with no confirmed compromise; low-risk malware alert automatically contained by endpoint protection. |

### 3.2 Severity Escalation

Incident severity may be increased at any time if:

- Additional systems or users become affected
- The incident spreads into OT or production environments
- Sensitive or personal data is exposed
- Safety risks emerge
- The attacker gains privileged access
- Business operations are significantly disrupted
- Legal, regulatory, or contractual notification obligations may apply

The Incident Response Manager has authority to change the severity classification.

## 4. Incident Response Team

GlobalTech maintains an Incident Response Team (IRT) responsible for coordinating security incident handling.

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| **Incident Response Manager** | Leads the response, assigns severity, coordinates teams, approves major containment actions, maintains overall incident status, and ensures required documentation is completed. |
| **Security Analysts** | Investigate alerts, collect and analyse evidence, identify indicators of compromise, determine scope, support containment and eradication, and monitor for attacker activity. |
| **IT Support / Infrastructure** | Isolate systems, restore services, apply patches, reset credentials, rebuild affected devices, and support recovery activities. |
| **OT / Manufacturing Engineering** | Assess impact on industrial systems, ensure containment actions do not create unsafe conditions, and coordinate recovery of OT/ICS and IoT systems. |
| **Legal Counsel / Data Protection Officer (DPO)** | Assess legal, contractual, GDPR, regulatory, and evidence-preservation obligations and advise on notifications. |
| **Communications / Public Relations** | Coordinates approved internal and external communications and prevents unauthorised or inconsistent public statements. |
| **Executive Sponsor** | Provides executive authority, supports major business decisions, approves extraordinary actions, and communicates with senior leadership. |
| **Human Resources** | Supports incidents involving employees, insider threats, disciplinary matters, or employee communications. |
| **Third-Party / Vendor Contacts** | Support incidents involving outsourced systems, cloud platforms, suppliers, or managed services. |

### 4.1 Incident Authority

For Critical and High incidents, the Incident Response Manager may request urgent actions including:

- Isolation of endpoints or servers
- Suspension of user accounts
- Blocking of malicious traffic
- Disconnection of affected network segments
- Temporary shutdown of affected services

Actions involving OT/ICS environments must be coordinated with OT / Manufacturing Engineering where immediate action could affect worker safety, equipment safety, or production integrity.

## 5. Detection and Reporting

### 5.1 Detection Sources

Security incidents may be detected through:

- Security Information and Event Management (SIEM)
- Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR)
- Antivirus or anti-malware alerts
- Network intrusion detection systems
- Firewall, VPN, and identity logs
- OT/ICS monitoring systems
- IoT monitoring
- Vulnerability management tools
- Cloud security alerts
- Employee or customer reports
- Third-party notifications
- Threat intelligence
- Law enforcement or regulator notifications

### 5.2 Employee Reporting

All employees and contractors must report suspected security incidents immediately.

Examples include:

- Suspicious emails or phishing
- Lost or stolen devices
- Unexpected MFA prompts
- Malware alerts
- Unauthorised access
- Accidental disclosure of confidential information
- Unusual behaviour on production or OT systems
- Suspected data theft
- Unexpected system changes
- Social engineering attempts

Reports must be made through the approved incident reporting channel, such as:

**Security Operations Centre (SOC) / IT Service Desk / Emergency Security Contact**

Critical incidents should be reported using the designated emergency contact method rather than relying only on email.

### 5.3 Information to Collect

Initial reports should include, where known:

- Name and contact information of the reporter
- Date and time the event was discovered
- Systems or devices affected
- User accounts involved
- Description of suspicious activity
- Error messages or alerts
- IP addresses, hostnames, or URLs
- Screenshots or logs where available
- Whether production, OT, safety, or personal data may be affected
- Actions already taken

Users must not investigate beyond their role if doing so could alter evidence or worsen the incident.

### 5.4 Initial Assessment

The Incident Response Team will:

1. Confirm whether the event is a security incident
2. Record the incident in the incident tracking system
3. Assign an initial severity level
4. Identify affected systems, accounts, locations, and data
5. Determine whether the incident is ongoing
6. Assess possible safety, operational, legal, and regulatory impact
7. Preserve relevant evidence
8. Escalate to appropriate stakeholders
9. Begin containment where required

## 6. Response Procedures

### 6.1 Preparation

GlobalTech must maintain incident response readiness through:

- Current incident response plans and contact lists
- Security monitoring and logging
- Secure backup systems
- Incident response tools
- Forensic capabilities
- Employee security awareness
- Incident response training
- Tabletop exercises
- OT/ICS-specific incident procedures
- Vendor and emergency contact information
- Tested recovery processes

The Incident Response Policy must be reviewed at least annually and after major incidents or significant organisational changes.

---

### 6.2 Detection and Analysis

Security Analysts must determine:

- What happened
- When the incident began
- How the incident was detected
- Which systems, accounts, or data are affected
- Whether the attacker still has access
- Whether the incident is spreading
- The likely attack vector
- Whether personal or regulated data is involved
- Whether OT, production, or safety systems are affected

All important findings and decisions must be documented in the incident record.

---

### 6.3 Containment

Containment aims to stop further damage while preserving evidence and maintaining safe operations.

#### Short-Term Containment

Possible actions include:

- Isolating compromised endpoints
- Disabling compromised accounts
- Blocking malicious IP addresses, domains, or file hashes
- Removing systems from the network
- Restricting remote access
- Segmenting affected network areas
- Blocking malicious email
- Temporarily disabling affected services

For OT/ICS systems, containment must consider worker safety, equipment safety, and production impact before disconnecting or shutting down industrial systems.

#### Evidence Preservation

Before destructive remediation where practical:

- Capture relevant logs
- Record system time
- Preserve volatile data where appropriate
- Capture memory or disk images where required
- Preserve suspicious files
- Record current network connections
- Document actions taken

#### Long-Term Containment

Long-term containment may include:

- Temporary network segmentation
- Increased monitoring
- Restricting privileged access
- Applying temporary firewall rules
- Replacing compromised credentials
- Moving critical services to clean infrastructure
- Implementing temporary compensating controls while permanent fixes are prepared

---

### 6.4 Eradication

Eradication removes the cause of the incident and attacker access.

Actions may include:

- Removing malware
- Deleting malicious accounts
- Removing persistence mechanisms
- Closing unauthorised remote access
- Patching exploited vulnerabilities
- Correcting misconfigurations
- Rotating compromised passwords, keys, tokens, and certificates
- Rebuilding compromised systems
- Removing unauthorised software
- Updating security controls

The Incident Response Team must identify the root cause where reasonably possible.

Before recovery, the team must validate that:

- Known malicious activity has been removed
- Compromised credentials have been reset
- Vulnerabilities used in the attack have been addressed
- Indicators of compromise are no longer present
- Affected systems are safe to restore

---

### 6.5 Recovery

Recovery returns systems and operations to normal service.

Recovery activities may include:

- Restoring clean backups
- Rebuilding systems
- Reconnecting systems in stages
- Testing system functionality
- Validating security controls
- Confirming data integrity
- Increasing security monitoring
- Monitoring for recurrence
- Returning production systems to service

Recovery of OT/ICS systems must be coordinated with Manufacturing Engineering and system owners.

Critical systems should be restored according to business continuity and disaster recovery priorities.

The Incident Response Manager must approve closure of Critical and High incidents after recovery criteria have been met.

## 7. Communication Plan

Incident communications must be accurate, controlled, timely, and based on verified information.

Only authorised personnel may communicate externally on behalf of GlobalTech.

### 7.1 Communication Matrix

| Stakeholder | When to Notify | Method |
|---|---|---|
| **Executive Management** | Immediately for Critical incidents; promptly for High incidents; when major business, safety, financial, or reputational impact is possible. | Secure phone call, approved messaging platform, executive incident briefing. |
| **Legal Counsel / DPO** | Immediately when personal data, legal obligations, contracts, litigation risk, evidence handling, or regulators may be involved. | Secure phone, approved messaging, incident briefing. |
| **Regulators / Supervisory Authorities** | When notification is legally or regulatorily required. GDPR personal data breaches must be assessed promptly so required notification can be made within the applicable legal timeframe. | Official regulator reporting process coordinated by Legal / DPO. |
| **Affected Users / Data Subjects** | When required by law, contract, or risk assessment, especially where the incident may create a high risk to individuals. | Approved email, customer notification, letter, portal notice, or other authorised communication. |
| **Employees** | When employees need instructions, protective actions, or operational updates. | Corporate email, internal messaging, intranet, emergency notification system. |
| **Customers / Business Partners** | When the incident affects their services, data, contractual obligations, or security. | Account management, approved email, formal notice. |
| **Law Enforcement** | When criminal activity is suspected and Legal / Executive Management approves engagement. | Official law-enforcement contact. |
| **Media / Public** | When public communication is necessary or the incident is publicly known. | Communications / PR-approved statement only. |

### 7.2 GDPR Notification

Where a personal data breach falls under GDPR:

- The Data Protection Officer and Legal Counsel must be notified immediately.
- The organisation must assess whether notification to the competent supervisory authority is required.
- Where required, notification must be made without undue delay and, where feasible, within **72 hours** of becoming aware of the breach.
- If notification is delayed beyond the required timeframe, the reason for the delay must be documented.
- Where the breach is likely to result in a high risk to individuals, affected data subjects must be informed without undue delay unless a lawful exception applies.

All GDPR decisions must be documented.

### 7.3 Communication Controls

Incident details must be shared only on a need-to-know basis.

Personnel must not:

- Post incident information on social media
- Discuss confidential incident details with unauthorised people
- Contact journalists independently
- Inform affected customers or regulators without approval
- Speculate publicly about cause, impact, or attribution

## 8. Evidence Handling

Evidence must be collected and preserved in a manner that supports investigation, legal review, disciplinary action, regulatory requirements, and possible law-enforcement use.

### 8.1 Chain of Custody

For collected evidence, the following must be documented:

- Unique evidence identifier
- Description of the evidence
- Source system or location
- Person who collected it
- Date and time collected
- Method of collection
- Hash value where applicable
- Storage location
- Every transfer of custody
- Name of each person accessing or receiving the evidence
- Date, time, and reason for each transfer

### 8.2 Evidence Preservation

Evidence must:

- Be preserved in its original form where possible
- Be protected from unauthorised access or modification
- Be stored in an approved secure location
- Use forensic copies for analysis when appropriate
- Be cryptographically hashed where applicable to support integrity verification
- Be retained according to legal, regulatory, contractual, and company retention requirements

Personnel should avoid actions that unnecessarily alter timestamps, logs, files, or volatile data.

### 8.3 Documentation

The incident record must document:

- Timeline of events
- Evidence collected
- Analysis performed
- Actions taken
- Persons involved
- Systems affected
- Important decisions
- Communications
- Containment and recovery activities
- Legal or regulatory assessments
- Final outcome

## 9. Post-Incident Activities

### 9.1 Lessons Learned

A lessons-learned review must be completed for:

- All Critical incidents
- All High incidents
- Significant Medium incidents
- Recurring incidents
- Incidents where response procedures did not work as expected

The review should normally occur after recovery while details are still current.

Participants should include relevant members of:

- Incident Response
- IT
- OT / Manufacturing
- Legal / DPO
- Business owners
- Communications
- Management
- Third-party providers where appropriate

The review should identify:

- What happened
- Root cause
- What worked well
- What did not work well
- Whether detection was timely
- Whether escalation was appropriate
- Whether communication was effective
- Whether evidence was preserved correctly
- What controls should be improved
- Whether policies or procedures need updating
- Follow-up actions, owners, and deadlines

### 9.2 Final Incident Report

A final report must be completed for Critical and High incidents.

The report must include:

- Executive summary
- Incident severity
- Timeline
- Systems and data affected
- Root cause
- Attack vector
- Business and operational impact
- Data or regulatory impact
- Containment actions
- Eradication actions
- Recovery actions
- Evidence summary
- Notifications made
- Lessons learned
- Corrective actions
- Assigned owners and deadlines

### 9.3 Corrective Actions

Corrective actions must be tracked until completion.

Examples include:

- Security patches
- Architecture changes
- Logging improvements
- Access-control changes
- User training
- Vendor remediation
- OT segmentation
- Backup improvements
- Updated detection rules
- Policy or procedure changes

## 10. Exceptions

### 10.1 Exception Process

Exceptions to this policy require:

1. Written request to the Information Security Team
2. Business justification
3. Risk assessment
4. Compensating controls where applicable
5. Legal / regulatory review if required
6. Formal approval by the Policy Owner or authorised risk owner

### 10.2 Exception Duration

All exceptions must:

- Have a defined end date
- Be documented
- Be reviewed at least annually
- Be removed when no longer necessary

No exception may override a mandatory legal or regulatory requirement.

## 12. Enforcement

### 12.1 Violations

Violations of this policy may result in:

- Verbal warning
- Written warning
- Mandatory security training
- Suspension of access privileges
- Disciplinary action up to and including termination
- Legal action where applicable

Examples of serious violations include:

- Deliberately failing to report a known incident
- Destroying or altering evidence
- Unauthorised disclosure of incident information
- Interfering with an active incident investigation
- Failing to follow approved containment instructions without valid safety or operational reason

### 12.2 Reporting Violations

Suspected violations must be reported to:

**Information Security Team / Incident Response Manager / IT Service Desk**

## 13. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Security Event** | An observable occurrence involving a system, network, service, or user that may or may not indicate a security problem. |
| **Security Incident** | A confirmed or suspected event that threatens the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of systems or data, or violates security policy. |
| **Containment** | Actions taken to limit the spread or impact of an incident. |
| **Eradication** | Removal of the threat, root cause, persistence, or vulnerability associated with an incident. |
| **Recovery** | Restoration of systems and business operations to a secure and normal state. |
| **OT** | Operational Technology used to monitor or control physical processes and industrial operations. |
| **ICS** | Industrial Control System used to manage or automate industrial processes. |
| **IoT** | Internet of Things devices that communicate over networks and may collect or process data. |
| **Chain of Custody** | A documented record showing the collection, possession, transfer, access, and handling of evidence. |
| **Personal Data Breach** | A security breach involving accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to personal data. |
