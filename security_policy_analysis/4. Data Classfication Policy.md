## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes requirements for classifying, labeling, storing, transmitting, accessing, and disposing of information handled by HealthPlus Medical Group.

The objective is to ensure that information receives protection appropriate to its sensitivity, business value, legal obligations, and potential impact if disclosed, altered, lost, or destroyed.

This policy supports the protection of patient medical records, employee information, financial information, research data, and business information and helps HealthPlus meet applicable HIPAA, GDPR, and state privacy requirements.

## 3. Classification Levels

All HealthPlus information must be assigned one of the following classification levels based on sensitivity and potential impact.

| Level | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **PUBLIC** | Information approved for public release. Unauthorised disclosure would cause little or no harm. | Public website content, approved marketing materials, public job postings, press releases |
| **INTERNAL** | Information intended for use within HealthPlus. Unauthorised disclosure may cause minor operational or business impact. | Internal memos, organisational charts, internal procedures, routine meeting notes |
| **CONFIDENTIAL** | Sensitive information where unauthorised disclosure, alteration, or loss could cause harm to individuals or HealthPlus. | Employee PII, financial data, contracts, non-public research data, sensitive business records |
| **RESTRICTED** | Highly sensitive information where unauthorised disclosure, alteration, or loss could cause severe harm, legal exposure, patient impact, or major operational damage. | PHI, ePHI, authentication credentials, highly sensitive personal data, encryption keys, selected regulated research data |

### 3.1 Classification Principles

- Data Owners are responsible for determining the appropriate classification.
- Information must be classified according to its **highest sensitivity element**.
- If multiple data types are combined, the combined dataset must use the highest applicable classification.
- Legal, regulatory, contractual, or patient privacy requirements may require a higher classification.
- When classification is uncertain, users must treat the information as **CONFIDENTIAL** until the Data Owner or Information Security / Privacy Office provides guidance.
- PHI and ePHI must normally be classified as **RESTRICTED**.
- Personal data and PII must normally be classified at least **CONFIDENTIAL**, unless the information has been lawfully and intentionally made public.
- Authentication credentials, encryption keys, and security secrets must be classified as **RESTRICTED**.

## 5. Policy Statements

### 5.1 Labeling

Information must be labeled in a manner that clearly communicates its classification to users.

#### PUBLIC

- A classification label is optional after formal approval for public release.
- Draft public-facing materials must remain INTERNAL or higher until approved.

#### INTERNAL

- Documents should include **INTERNAL** in the header, footer, document properties, or approved metadata where practical.
- File names may include the classification, for example:  
  `INTERNAL_Staff_Meeting_Notes.docx`

#### CONFIDENTIAL

- Documents must be clearly marked **CONFIDENTIAL**.
- The label should appear in the header, footer, document banner, or approved metadata.
- File names should include the classification where supported, for example:  
  `CONFIDENTIAL_Employee_Report.xlsx`

#### RESTRICTED

- Documents must be clearly marked **RESTRICTED**.
- Electronic systems should apply classification metadata or automated labels where supported.
- File names should include the classification where practical, for example:  
  `RESTRICTED_Patient_Record_Export.csv`
- Labels must not reveal unnecessary PHI or sensitive personal information in file names.

---

### 5.2 Storage

Information must only be stored in locations approved for its classification.

#### PUBLIC

Approved storage includes:

- Public websites
- Approved marketing platforms
- Approved public repositories
- Corporate storage systems

#### INTERNAL

Approved storage includes:

- Corporate file shares
- Approved cloud storage
- Approved collaboration tools
- Company-managed devices

INTERNAL information must not be stored in personal cloud accounts or personal email.

#### CONFIDENTIAL

CONFIDENTIAL information must:

- Be stored only in approved company systems
- Be encrypted at rest
- Be protected by role-based access controls
- Be accessible only to personnel with a legitimate business need
- Not be stored on unapproved portable media
- Not be copied to personal devices or personal cloud services

#### RESTRICTED

RESTRICTED information must:

- Be stored only in specifically approved systems
- Be encrypted at rest
- Use strong access controls and least privilege
- Be protected by logging and monitoring
- Be stored on portable media only when specifically authorised and encrypted
- Not be stored on unapproved personal devices
- Not be stored in personal email, consumer cloud storage, or unauthorised collaboration services

PHI and ePHI must only be stored in systems approved for healthcare information.

---

### 5.3 Transmission

Information must be transmitted using methods appropriate to its classification.

#### PUBLIC

- May be transmitted through normal approved communication methods once formally approved for release.

#### INTERNAL

- Must use company-approved email, collaboration platforms, or file-transfer tools.
- External sharing requires a legitimate business purpose.

#### CONFIDENTIAL

- Must be encrypted in transit.
- Email containing CONFIDENTIAL information must use approved secure email controls where required.
- Files must be shared through approved secure file-transfer or collaboration platforms.
- Recipients must be verified before transmission.
- External recipients must have an authorised business need.

#### RESTRICTED

- Must be encrypted in transit using approved secure protocols or platforms.
- PHI and other RESTRICTED information must not be sent through personal email, consumer messaging services, or unapproved file-sharing platforms.
- Recipient identity and authorisation must be verified.
- Sensitive files should use approved secure portals, encrypted transfer methods, or secure healthcare communication systems.
- External transmission must follow applicable privacy, contractual, and regulatory requirements.
- Users must confirm that the minimum necessary information is being shared.

---

### 5.4 Disposal

Information must be disposed of securely when it is no longer required and when retention obligations permit disposal.

#### Paper Records

- PUBLIC material may be recycled or disposed of normally when appropriate.
- INTERNAL documents containing non-public information should be placed in approved confidential waste containers where appropriate.
- CONFIDENTIAL and RESTRICTED paper documents must be cross-cut shredded or placed in approved locked secure-destruction containers.
- Destruction services must use approved vendors where outsourced.

#### Electronic Media

Electronic media containing company information must be sanitised before reuse, transfer, or disposal.

Sanitisation methods must be selected based on the sensitivity of the information and the media type.

Approved methods may include:

- **Clear** - logical techniques appropriate when media will remain within controlled environments
- **Purge** - stronger sanitisation techniques designed to make recovery infeasible using advanced methods
- **Destroy** - physical destruction when media cannot be safely reused or when risk requires destruction

For CONFIDENTIAL and RESTRICTED information:

- Simple file deletion is not sufficient.
- Media sanitisation must use an approved method.
- RESTRICTED media must be purged or destroyed where required by risk, media type, or applicable requirements.
- Disposal must be documented where required.

---

### 5.5 Access Control

Access must be based on business need, job responsibilities, and the sensitivity of the information.

#### PUBLIC

- No access restriction is required after formal approval for public release.
- Only authorised personnel may modify official public information.

#### INTERNAL

- Access is limited to authenticated HealthPlus personnel and authorised third parties.
- Access should be based on business need.

#### CONFIDENTIAL

- Access must follow role-based access control and need-to-know principles.
- Managers or Data Owners must approve access.
- Access must be reviewed at least annually.
- Access must be removed promptly when no longer required.

#### RESTRICTED

- Access must follow strict least privilege and need-to-know principles.
- Access must be specifically authorised by the Data Owner or designated authority.
- MFA must be used where required by HealthPlus security standards.
- Access to systems containing RESTRICTED data must be logged.
- High-risk access must be monitored.
- Access rights must be reviewed at least annually and more frequently for high-risk systems where appropriate.
- Access must be removed immediately when a user leaves the organisation or no longer requires access.

## 7. Compliance

### 7.1 Monitoring

Compliance may be monitored through:

- Access logs
- Data Loss Prevention (DLP) tools
- Cloud security monitoring
- Email security controls
- Access reviews
- Security incident monitoring
- Data discovery and classification tools
- Audit results

### 7.2 Reporting

Users must immediately report:

- Suspected data loss
- Accidental disclosure
- Misclassification
- Unauthorised access
- Transmission to the wrong recipient
- Loss of a device containing HealthPlus information
- Storage of sensitive information in an unapproved location

Reports must be made to:

**Information Security Team / Privacy Office / IT Service Desk**

Potential breaches involving PHI or personal data must be escalated according to the Incident Response Policy and applicable breach-response procedures.

### 7.3 Auditing

Data handling controls must be reviewed periodically.

Audits may verify:

- Classification accuracy
- Storage locations
- Encryption controls
- Access permissions
- External sharing
- Retention and disposal
- PHI/ePHI safeguards
- Third-party handling
- Compliance with privacy obligations

---

## 8. Exceptions

### 8.1 Exception Process

Exceptions require:

1. Written request to the Information Security / Privacy Office
2. Business justification
3. Identification of affected data and classification
4. Risk assessment
5. Compensating controls where applicable
6. Privacy or Legal review where regulated information is involved
7. Formal approval and documentation

### 8.2 Exception Duration

All exceptions must:

- Have a defined end date
- Be reviewed at least annually
- Be removed when no longer required

Exceptions may not override mandatory legal or regulatory requirements.

## 9. Enforcement

### 9.1 Violations

Violations may result in:

- Verbal warning
- Written warning
- Mandatory privacy or security training
- Restriction or suspension of access
- Removal of system privileges
- Disciplinary action up to and including termination
- Contractual action for third parties
- Legal or regulatory action where applicable

Serious violations include:

- Deliberate disclosure of PHI or personal information without authorisation
- Storing RESTRICTED information in unapproved systems
- Circumventing access controls
- Sending confidential information to personal accounts
- Improper disposal of sensitive information

### 9.2 Reporting Violations

Suspected violations must be reported to:

**Information Security Team / Privacy Office / IT Service Desk**

## 10. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Data Classification** | The process of categorising information according to sensitivity, value, risk, and protection requirements. |
| **PHI** | Protected Health Information; individually identifiable health information protected under HIPAA when applicable. |
| **ePHI** | Protected Health Information created, received, maintained, or transmitted electronically. |
| **PII** | Personally Identifiable Information that can identify or reasonably be linked to an individual. |
| **Personal Data** | Information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person under GDPR. |
| **Data Owner** | The person or business role responsible for determining classification and approving appropriate access to information. |
| **Data Custodian** | A person or technical team responsible for implementing controls that protect information. |
| **Encryption at Rest** | Protection of stored data using approved encryption. |
| **Encryption in Transit** | Protection of information while it travels between systems or users. |
| **Media Sanitisation** | A process that renders access to data stored on media infeasible for a given level of effort. |
| **Least Privilege** | Granting users only the minimum access required to perform their authorised duties. |
| **Need-to-Know** | Restricting access to information to individuals who require it for a legitimate business or healthcare purpose. |

# Appendix A - Quick Reference Guide

## HealthPlus Data Classification - Employee Quick Reference

Use this guide when creating, storing, sharing, or disposing of HealthPlus information.

### Classification Overview

| Classification | Use When | Examples | Key Protection |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PUBLIC** | Information has been approved for public release | Marketing material, website content, public job postings | Protect integrity; no sensitive information |
| **INTERNAL** | Information is for normal internal business use | Internal memos, organisation charts, procedures | Company systems only; authenticated access |
| **CONFIDENTIAL** | Disclosure could harm a person or HealthPlus | Employee PII, financial records, contracts, non-public research | Encrypt, restrict access, secure sharing |
| **RESTRICTED** | Disclosure could cause severe harm or major legal/privacy impact | PHI, ePHI, credentials, encryption keys | Strong encryption, strict least privilege, logging |

### Before You Handle Data

Ask:

1. **What type of information is this?**
2. **Would disclosure harm a patient, employee, or HealthPlus?**
3. **Does it contain PHI, personal information, credentials, or other regulated data?**
4. **Who actually needs access?**
5. **Am I using an approved system?**

If unsure, treat the information as **CONFIDENTIAL** and ask the Data Owner, Privacy Office, or Information Security Team.

### Quick Handling Rules

| Action | PUBLIC | INTERNAL | CONFIDENTIAL | RESTRICTED |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Label** | Optional after approval | INTERNAL | CONFIDENTIAL | RESTRICTED |
| **Store in company systems** | Recommended | Required | Required | Required |
| **Encrypt at rest** | Not required | Recommended | Required | Required |
| **Encrypt in transit** | Recommended | Required | Required | Required |
| **Send to personal email** | Only if already public | Never | Never | Never |
| **Use personal cloud storage** | Only for already public material where permitted | Never | Never | Never |
| **External sharing** | Allowed after public approval | Business need | Approval + secure method | Strict approval + secure method |
| **Secure disposal** | Normal when appropriate | Secure disposal | Secure disposal | Enhanced secure disposal |

### Remember

- **PHI = RESTRICTED**
- **Credentials and encryption keys = RESTRICTED**
- **Employee PII and financial information = normally CONFIDENTIAL**
- Never move sensitive HealthPlus data to personal email, personal cloud storage, or unapproved devices.
- Always verify recipients before sending CONFIDENTIAL or RESTRICTED information.
- Use approved secure systems for PHI and other RESTRICTED information.
- Do not put unnecessary patient information in file names.
- Securely dispose of sensitive paper and electronic media.
- Report accidental disclosure, lost devices, wrong-recipient emails, or suspected data exposure immediately.
