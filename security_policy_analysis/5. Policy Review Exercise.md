# Policy Review Exercise

## RetailMax Corporation Security Policy Program Assessment

---

## Executive Summary

RetailMax Corporation is preparing for PCI DSS certification, ISO/IEC 27001 certification, and a SOC 2 Type II audit. Based on the policy inventory provided, the organisation has a partially established security policy program, but it is not currently mature enough to support audit readiness without significant remediation.

RetailMax currently maintains three active security policies:

- Information Security Policy — last issued or updated in **2019**
- Password Policy — last issued or updated in **2020**
- Acceptable Use Policy — last issued or updated in **2021**

Three important policies are missing:

- Incident Response Policy
- Data Classification Policy
- Access Control Policy

The existing policies are between five and seven years old. Even if their original content was appropriate, their age creates a significant governance concern because security requirements, business systems, threats, and compliance expectations have changed. PCI DSS requires security policies and operational procedures to be documented, kept up to date, in use, and known to affected personnel. RetailMax therefore needs both new policies and a formal policy lifecycle that includes ownership, approval, review, communication, evidence of implementation, and periodic measurement.

The most urgent policy gaps relate to **incident response, access control, data handling, and the age of the Information Security Policy**. PCI DSS Requirement 12 includes the organisation's information security policy and incident response program, while Requirements 7 and 8 address access control and authentication. Data classification is also important because RetailMax must know where account data exists, who may access it, how it is handled, and how it is protected.

The current maturity assessment places the three existing policies at **Level 2 – Developing** because they are documented but there is no evidence that they are current, consistently communicated, measured, or continuously improved. The three missing policies are assessed at **Level 0 – Non-existent**.

RetailMax should use the next 12 months to establish a complete policy framework, remediate the highest-risk gaps, implement evidence-producing compliance processes, and complete readiness assessments before external certification or audit activity. The recommended target is to move critical policies to at least **Level 3 – Defined** during the first six months and selected high-risk areas to **Level 4 – Managed** by the end of the year.

> **Assessment limitation:** This review is based only on the provided policy inventory and policy dates. The actual policy documents, technical configurations, control evidence, audit logs, training records, and operational procedures were not provided. Therefore, this assessment identifies **policy-program gaps and likely compliance risks**, not definitive technical non-compliance.

## 2. High-Priority PCI Policy Gaps

### 2.1 Incident Response

**Current state:** No Incident Response Policy exists.

**Why this matters:** RetailMax needs a documented process for detecting, reporting, containing, investigating, recovering from, and learning from security incidents. A payment-card compromise without an established response plan could increase business impact and delay required escalation.

**Gap rating:** **Critical**

**Required action:**

- Create an Incident Response Policy
- Define severity levels and escalation paths
- Assign an Incident Response Team
- Create contact and communication procedures
- Include payment-data compromise scenarios
- Define evidence handling
- Conduct incident response exercises
- Review and test the plan regularly

---

### 2.2 Access Control

**Current state:** No Access Control Policy exists.

**Why this matters:** PCI DSS requires access to system components and account data to be restricted according to business need and appropriate privileges.

**Gap rating:** **Critical**

**Required action:**

- Create an Access Control Policy
- Apply least privilege
- Define role-based access
- Require management approval for access
- Define privileged account controls
- Conduct periodic access reviews
- Remove access promptly after role changes or termination
- Document account provisioning and deprovisioning

---

### 2.3 Data Classification and Handling

**Current state:** No Data Classification Policy exists.

**Why this matters:** RetailMax cannot consistently protect payment data if users do not know which information is sensitive and what handling requirements apply.

**Gap rating:** **Critical**

**Required action:**

Create classification levels that clearly identify:

- Public data
- Internal business information
- Confidential data
- Restricted/payment account data

The policy should define:

- Storage
- Encryption
- Transmission
- Retention
- Access
- Media handling
- Disposal
- External sharing

---

### 2.4 Information Security Policy

**Current state:** Active, dated 2019.

**Why this matters:** The document predates PCI DSS 4.x, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, NIST CSF 2.0, and the organisation's current certification effort.

**Gap rating:** **High**

**Required action:**

- Perform a full review rather than simply changing the date
- Update scope, responsibilities, regulatory references, governance, risk management, and policy ownership
- Obtain executive approval
- Establish a minimum annual review cycle
- Maintain revision history
- Communicate changes to personnel

---

### 2.5 Password Policy

**Current state:** Active, dated 2020.

**Gap rating:** **High**

The policy should be reassessed against current authentication requirements, including:

- Password/passphrase requirements
- MFA
- Account lockout and rate limiting
- Privileged accounts
- Service accounts
- Authentication credential storage
- Password reset and recovery
- Account lifecycle controls
- Current PCI DSS Requirement 8

---

### 2.6 Acceptable Use Policy

**Current state:** Active, dated 2021.

**Gap rating:** **Medium**

The policy should be reviewed for:

- Remote work
- Cloud services
- Personal devices/BYOD
- Approved software
- Corporate email
- Internet use
- Social media
- Data handling
- Generative AI and other newly adopted technologies, where applicable
- User monitoring and privacy expectations

## 3. Cross-Framework Impact

Although Part A focuses on PCI DSS, the same policy gaps affect the other planned assurance activities.

| Gap | PCI DSS Impact | ISO/IEC 27001 Impact | SOC 2 Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outdated Information Security Policy | Weakens Requirement 12 governance | Weakens the ISMS policy/governance structure | Weakens evidence of governance and control environment |
| Missing Incident Response Policy | Direct incident-response gap | Weakens incident management controls | Affects Security and potentially Availability criteria |
| Missing Access Control Policy | Direct Requirements 7/8 risk | Weakens access control governance | Affects logical access/security controls |
| Missing Data Classification Policy | Weakens consistent protection of account data | Weakens information classification/handling controls | Affects Confidentiality and Security controls |
| No demonstrated policy review process | Policies may become obsolete | Conflicts with continual improvement approach | Makes sustained control operation harder to demonstrate |
| No evidence of monitoring/metrics | Compliance cannot be demonstrated consistently | Weakens performance evaluation | Particularly problematic for a Type II audit, which evaluates controls over a period of time |

ISO/IEC 27001 is built around establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an Information Security Management System (ISMS). SOC 2 similarly requires organisations to demonstrate that relevant controls are designed and, for a Type II engagement, operate over the audit period. RetailMax therefore needs more than written policies: it needs evidence that the policies are implemented.

Create both a high-level policy and operational procedures.

- Incident classification
- Response roles

- Exercises and testing

# Conclusion

RetailMax is not starting from zero: it already has an Information Security Policy, Password Policy, and Acceptable Use Policy. However, the current program is incomplete and the existing documents are too old to provide strong evidence of an actively governed security program.

The immediate objective should not simply be to produce more documents. RetailMax should establish a **policy lifecycle** in which requirements are approved, communicated, implemented, monitored, reviewed, and improved.

The highest-priority actions are to:

1. Create the missing Incident Response, Access Control, and Data Classification policies
2. Modernise the existing Information Security, Password, and Acceptable Use policies
3. Establish formal policy ownership and annual review
4. Translate policy requirements into technical standards and operational procedures
5. Collect evidence that controls operate consistently
6. Perform internal readiness reviews before external PCI DSS, ISO 27001, and SOC 2 assessment

Completing the 12-month roadmap should place RetailMax in a substantially stronger position for all three assurance goals while also creating a more sustainable security governance program.
